My research uses geological records and quantitative approaches to investigate deep-time Earth system evolution, with particular emphasis on Earth–Moon dynamics, astronomical forcing, and large-scale geoscientific data.

Research Topics

Astrochronology & Cyclostratigraphy

I use quantitative methods to identify astronomical signals in geological records. By analyzing cyclostratigraphic depth series, I investigate how orbital forcing is preserved in sediments and how these signals can help constrain geological time.

Earth–Moon Evolution

I combine geological evidence with tidal simulation models to investigate the evolution of the Earth–Moon system. My research on Earth's axial precession frequency explores the connections between astronomical dynamics, tidal processes, and deep-time Earth system evolution.

Related publications →

Zircon Geochronology and Long-Term Earth System Evolution

I use global zircon U–Th–Pb geochronological records and time-series analysis to investigate long-term patterns in continental crust evolution and Earth system evolution, including their possible links to astronomical forcing.
