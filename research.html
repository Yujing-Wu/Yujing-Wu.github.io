My research uses geological records and quantitative approaches to investigate deep-time Earth system evolution, with particular emphasis on Earth–Moon dynamics, astronomical forcing, and large-scale geoscientific data.

Research Topics

Astronomical Time Scales from Geological Records

My current research focuses on extracting eccentricity signals from stratigraphic records and reconstructing astronomical time scales for individual geological datasets. By applying AstroGeoFit across records spanning 40–100 Ma, I aim to build a consistent geological framework that can provide independent constraints for evaluating deep-time astronomical solutions.

Earth–Moon System Evolution

I use cyclostratigraphic records to reconstruct Earth’s axial precession frequency and constrain the long-term evolution of the Earth–Moon system. These geological records provide independent estimates of lunar distance, length of day, and tidal dissipation, offering a deep-time perspective on planetary dynamics and astronomical forcing.

Zircon Geochronology and Long-Term Earth System Evolution

I use global zircon U–Th–Pb geochronological records and time-series analysis to investigate long-term patterns in continental crust evolution and Earth system evolution, including their possible links to astronomical forcing.
