Biography

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Paris Observatory. My study is about identifying astronomical signals from cyclostratigraphic depth series. My recent research was centered on reconstructing Earth's axial precession frequency evolution to better understand the astronomical influences on Earth system processes, with a particular focus on tidal effects on Earth's interior and surface. I aimed to combine geological evidence with tidal simulation models to gain deeper insights into the evolution of Earth's systems and their connection to broader Solar System dynamics. Throughout my Ph.D. research, I focused on exploring the evolution of Earth's crust, major geological events, and the periodicity within Earth's systems through time-series analysis of zircon U-Pb geochronology data. I was dedicated to big earth data and geological data analysis because the integrated analysis of widely distributed geological records yields important information about the earth system that cannot be obtained by studying these records individually.

Current Research

Astronomical Time Scales from Geological Records

My current research focuses on reconstructing absolute astronomical time scales from geological records using AstroGeoFit. I extract orbital signals and optimize age–depth relationships to build quantitative chronologies for stratigraphic datasets across a wide range of geological intervals, with broader applications to evaluating deep-time astronomical solutions.

My expertise in AstroGeoFit has already led to active collaborations on astronomical time-scale reconstruction from stratigraphic series across different geological intervals, and I welcome further collaborations using the framework on both new and existing geological records.
